Transit Card Research

Research date: 2026-05-09. These notes capture public implementation checks and CoreExtendedNFC decisions behind transit-card balance work.

Japan IC

  • FeliCa system code: 0003.
  • Balance service: 008B, encoded for CoreNFC as 8B 00.
  • Balance bytes: block bytes 11-12, little-endian, JPY.

Hong Kong Octopus

  • FeliCa system code: 8008.
  • Balance service: 0117, encoded as 17 01.
  • Formula in HKD cents: (raw - 350) * 10.

China T-Union

  • AID: A000000632010105.
  • Primary purse APDU: 80 5C 00 02.
  • Negative purse APDU: 80 5C 01 02.
  • Final balance: primary purse minus negative purse, CNY fen.

KS X 6924

CoreExtendedNFC probes Hyundai, T-Money, Cashbee, EB Card, Snapper / MOIBA, and K-Cash AIDs and uses balance APDU 90 4C 00 00.

Singapore CEPAS

Experimental implementation added 2026-09-25. Traditional EZ-Link purse and recent logs are read from the card; SimplyGo account-based history is outside this reader. On that date, one traditional EZ-Link card was read successfully on iPhone Air running iOS 27.0 (24A5355q). The app displayed its balance and four records: two journeys, one top-up, and one entry displayed as unknown. The result has not yet been compared with a ticket-machine reference, repeated offline, or verified on other card variants.

The example app includes A000000341000101 as an existing unverified AID candidate. No reliable CEPAS source has been established for this value, and the actual initial AID from the successful read has not been confirmed. The single-card result therefore does not verify this candidate. Core NFC must first expose a compatible ISO 7816 tag; the dedicated scan avoids application-refinement probes before the CEPAS read.

GET PURSE, purse 390 32 03 00 00
GET HISTORY, purse 390 32 03 00 01 <offset> <Le>

Both reads use instruction 32. History adds one data byte for the record offset and requests at most 15 records per batch, with 16 bytes per record.

A direct iOS Simulator check of NFCISO7816APDU(data:) accepted the standard five-byte GET PURSE command 90 32 03 00 00, but returned nil for 90 32 03 00 00 00. The reader therefore uses the existing structured APDU transport and the five-byte command. The subsequent single-card iPhone Air test also completed the five-byte GET PURSE and history commands successfully. The simulator check alone does not establish physical-card compatibility.

Metrodroid’s historical six-byte GET PURSE fix records a compatibility issue with the shorter form. Older cards may return 6700 to the standard encoding used here. Preserve that failure in Logs; do not report such a card as supported.

Follow the physical-card verification steps and retain the existing Logs export for both successes and failures. Mock data checks parser behavior, not iOS detection or card support.
