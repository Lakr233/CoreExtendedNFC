Transit Card Research

Research date: 2026-05-09. These notes capture public implementation checks and CoreExtendedNFC decisions behind transit-card balance work.

Japan IC

  • FeliCa system code: 0003.
  • Balance service: 008B, encoded for CoreNFC as 8B 00.
  • Balance bytes: block bytes 11-12, little-endian, JPY.

Regional Japanese Cards

Implementation checked on 2026-10-31 against CardBal's FeliCa profiles and TRETJapanNFCReader (MIT). Its FeliCaSystemCode identifies IruCa as 80DE, PASPY as 8592, SAPICA as 865E and OKICA as 8FC1. TransitICCardItemType and TransitICCardData describe the public balance and history services and SAPICA's three-byte little-endian points field in BA4B.

OKICA uses its own 028F history service. OkicaCardItemType describes 20 slots; OkicaCardData describes operation codes in bytes 0-3, a packed date in bytes 4-5, entry and exit locations in bytes 6-9, little-endian balance in bytes 10-11 and a big-endian sequence number in bytes 13-14. Balance deltas are inferred from adjacent history slots. The implementation keeps the oldest populated record with no amount, skips empty slots and retains unknown operations with their raw data.

OKICA was tested on an ACR122U on 2026-10-31. System enumeration returned 8FC1 and FE00; the card did not answer 0003. The 028F capture contains two populated slots and eighteen empty ones: a 250-yen balance after a Yui Rail journey, preceded by 500-yen issuance. These records are replayed through automatic detection, balance and history parsing, with a replacement IDm. IruCa, PASPY, SAPICA and SAPICA points have synthetic parser and service tests.

Hong Kong Octopus

  • FeliCa system code: 8008.
  • Balance service: 0117, encoded as 17 01.
  • Formula in HKD cents: (raw - 350) * 10, the offset used by CardBal and Y Mobile for live reads.
  • Metrodroid and FareBot use an offset of 500 for cards read after 2017-10-01. Which offset applies to current physical cards has not been verified. OctopusReader(transport:balanceOffset:) lets callers override the offset.

China T-Union

  • AID: A000000632010105.
  • Primary purse APDU: 80 5C 00 02.
  • Negative purse APDU: 80 5C 01 02.
  • Final balance: primary purse minus negative purse, CNY fen. Metrodroid shows the primary purse whenever it is positive, but a logged Shanghai card with 800 fen in both purses showed CNY 0.00 in the issuer app, which matches the plain difference.
  • A status-word rejection of the negative-purse command means there is no negative purse (0). Session loss and other failures are propagated.

Singapore CEPAS

Implementation added 2026-09-25. Traditional EZ-Link purse and recent logs are read from the card; SimplyGo account-based history is outside this reader. On that date, one traditional EZ-Link card was read successfully on iPhone Air running iOS 27.0 (24A5355q). The app displayed its balance and four records: two journeys, one top-up, and one entry displayed as unknown. This test used the dedicated Singapore reader. The integrated Scan Transit Card flow has not yet been verified on a physical card. The result has not yet been compared with a ticket-machine reference, repeated offline, or verified on other card variants.

The example app includes A000000341000101 as an existing unverified AID candidate. No reliable CEPAS source has been established for this value, and the actual initial AID from the successful read has not been confirmed. The single-card result therefore does not verify this candidate. Core NFC must first expose a compatible ISO 7816 tag. Transit scanning passes the original NFCSessionManager.scan result to readTransitBalance without application refinement. On ISO 7816 tags, SELECT file 4000 and GET PURSE probe CEPAS before the KS X 6924 and T-Union readers; only recognized non-matching responses permit fallback.

OperationEncoded command
SELECT CEPAS file00 A4 00 00 02 40 00
GET PURSE, purse 390 32 03 00 00
GET HISTORY, purse 390 32 03 00 01 <offset> <Le>

Both reads use instruction 32. History adds one data byte for the record offset and requests at most 15 records per batch, with 16 bytes per record. CEPAS timestamps and dates count from 1995-01-01 00:00 in Singapore (UTC+08:00), i.e. 1994-12-31T16:00:00Z, the same epoch as FareBot and Metrodroid.

A direct iOS Simulator check of NFCISO7816APDU(data:) accepted the standard five-byte GET PURSE command 90 32 03 00 00, but returned nil for 90 32 03 00 00 00. The reader therefore uses the existing structured APDU transport and the five-byte command. The subsequent single-card iPhone Air test also completed the five-byte GET PURSE and history commands successfully. The simulator check alone does not establish physical-card compatibility.

Metrodroid’s historical six-byte GET PURSE fix records a compatibility issue with the shorter form. Older cards may return 6700 to the standard encoding used here. Preserve that failure in Logs; do not report such a card as supported.

Follow the physical-card verification steps and retain the existing Logs export for both successes and failures. Mock data checks parser behavior, not iOS detection or card support.
