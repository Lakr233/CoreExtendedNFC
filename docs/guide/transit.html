Transit Cards

Transit balance readers probe known public application paths and return a normalized TransitBalance.

let manager = NFCSessionManager()
defer { manager.invalidate() }
let (info, transport) = try await manager.scan()
let balance = try await CoreExtendedNFC.readTransitBalance(info: info, transport: transport)

Pass the original info and transport from NFCSessionManager.scan directly to the reader. Do not call refineCardInfo first: its probes can change the selected application. CoreExtendedNFC.scan performs that refinement automatically, so use NFCSessionManager.scan for transit reads.

FeliCa cards are selected by system code. ISO 7816 cards are probed for CEPAS with SELECT file 4000 and GET PURSE, then for KS X 6924 and T-Union.

Detection Fallback

  • CEPAS: any status-word rejection of SELECT 4000, or GET PURSE answered with 6A81, 6A82, 6D00 or 6E00, means "not CEPAS" and the next reader runs. Communication, malformed-response and history errors are thrown.
  • KS X 6924 and T-Union: only NFCError.applicationNotFound(aid:) (no AID selectable) or .tagNotSupported moves on. Once an AID is selected, a rejected GET BALANCE or any other failure is thrown.
  • Session loss and task cancellation always end the read. If no reader recognizes the card, NFCError.tagNotSupported is thrown.

Regional Japanese Cards

readTransitBalance recognizes IruCa, PASPY, SAPICA and OKICA by their selected FeliCa system code. Register the relevant codes under com.apple.developer.nfc.readersession.felica.systemcodes in Info.plist. The example app registers all four.

JapanICReader also accepts 80DE, 8592 and 865E, returning the regional card name. SAPICA's public BA4B block supplies metadata["points"] as a three-byte little-endian integer. A refused points read leaves it out; session loss and cancellation propagate.

OKICAReader.readBalance() reads the latest 028F transaction, whose bytes 10-11 hold the balance in yen. readBalanceAndHistory(), also exposed as CoreExtendedNFC.readOKICABalance(transport:), reads up to 20 slots. A refused latest record fails the read; a refused later record ends the history. Empty slots are skipped. Dates are JST.

OKICA history keeps sequence numbers and raw records. Yui Rail journeys carry entry and exit station codes with region 3 and metadata["mode"] == "monorail". Bus journeys carry metadata["mode"] == "bus" and raw entryLocationCode / exitLocationCode. Charges, point exchanges and issuance are top-ups. Known trips and top-ups infer an unsigned amount from the adjacent older populated slot when the balance moves in the expected direction; the oldest slot and unknown operations have no amount.

OKICA was tested with an ACR122U on 2026-10-31. The capture is replayed in package tests: a 250-yen balance, a 250-yen Yui Rail journey and a 500-yen issuance record. The fixture replaces the IDm. IruCa, PASPY and SAPICA have synthetic service and routing tests.

Amounts and Balances

  • TransitTransaction.amount is always an unsigned magnitude. Use type for direction: .topup adds, .trip and .purchase deduct.
  • T-Union: the displayed balance is the primary purse minus the negative purse, in fen. A card that rejects the negative-purse command with a status word has no negative purse (treated as 0); other failures are thrown. Transaction types 01 and 02 are top-ups.
  • Snapper: Wellington Snapper cards answer the T-Money AID. When every SFI 4 record carries Snapper's FF filler, the card is reported as Snapper in NZD cents. Snapper trip records are not decoded.
  • Octopus: balance in HKD cents is (raw − 350) × 10. Sources disagree on the offset (Metrodroid and FareBot use 500 for scans after 2017-10-01), and 350 has not been confirmed on a current physical card; create OctopusReader(transport:balanceOffset:) to use another offset. See the offset note.
  • CEPAS: timestamps count from 1995-01-01 00:00 Singapore time (UTC+08:00). Transaction types follow Metrodroid: 30 / 31 (MRT, bus) are trips, 03 / 75 top-ups and 76 (bus refund) a top-up, 01 (retail) and 04 (service charge) purchases, each with its amount. Other types keep their raw payload and have no amount.
  • Japan IC history follows Metrodroid SuicaTrip: rail gate records have an entry and an exit station; ticket-machine and fare-adjustment records (console types 03, 07, 08, 12-15) have only entryStation, and exitStation is nil; bus records (console type 05) have no stations and carry the line and stop codes in metadata["busLineCode"] and metadata["busStopCode"]. Records with stations also carry the region of each station (byte 15: entry bits 7-6, exit bits 5-4) in metadata["entryRegion"] and metadata["exitRegion"] ("0"-"3"); a station code is unique only together with its region. Product sales and charges carry no stations. Process types 02h, 07h, 14h, 15h, 17h (bus auto-charge), 1Fh, 48h and 49h are top-ups. History slots whose date field (bytes 4-5) is zero are skipped, as in Metrodroid SuicaTransitData; records with an out-of-range date or purchase time are kept with date == nil.

Singapore EZ-Link / CEPAS

Traditional, non-SimplyGo CEPAS cards are included in readTransitBalance and the example app’s Scan Transit Card action. Reads return the purse and recent transaction log. A single traditional EZ-Link card was read successfully on iPhone Air running iOS 27.0 (24A5355q) on 2026-09-25: the app displayed its balance and four history records. That test used the dedicated Singapore path; the integrated automatic scan has not yet been tested on a physical card. Offline operation, ticket-machine comparison, and other card variants have not been verified. SimplyGo account-based history is not supported.

The example app’s ISO 7816 AID list already contains A000000341000101 as an existing unverified candidate. The actual initial AID from the successful read has not been confirmed, so that read does not verify this candidate. Preserve the initial AID in exported Logs when testing another card.

The reader uses the standard five-byte GET PURSE encoding accepted by Core NFC and the tested card. Older cards may reject it with 6700; the historical six-byte variant is rejected by NFCISO7816APDU(data:) in the iOS Simulator. See the protocol findings.

Physical Card Verification

  1. Use one traditional EZ-Link card that has not been upgraded to SimplyGo. Photograph or record its ticket-machine balance and two known recent journeys, including their dates, times, amounts, and stations or bus routes.
  2. Run the example app on an NFC-capable iPhone. Turn off Wi-Fi and cellular data, then open Tools → Transit Balance → Scan Transit Card. Keep only that card near the phone.
  3. Compare the displayed balance and recent records with the reference. Singapore transaction times are shown in Singapore time and monetary amounts in SGD. Raw monetary values use SGD cents. Records without a recognized monetary meaning retain their raw type and payload but omit an amount. CEPAS logs do not provide a post-transaction balance, so it is omitted.
  4. Open Logs and export the existing text/JSON log bundle. It includes session outcome, initial AID when detected, and APDU traffic. Record the iPhone model, iOS version, card type, and reference journeys with the test result.
  5. Record detection failures, protocol errors, and mismatches as failures. Distinguish a successful read from a fully verified result: an offline read must match the reference balance and both known journeys before marking that comparison complete. Simulator and mock tests cannot establish physical-card support.
